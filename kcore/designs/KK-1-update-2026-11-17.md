## 5. `hdef` (replaces the paragraph "What `hdef` asks")

`hdef : Eval K₀ sym.succs [embD E, emb (enc x.val)] (some s)`: one call of `kore.k`'s evaluation has a value. With the ascending walk landed, beyond what `Step (d₀ d) x y` asks it asks:
- of every rule of the least priority at which a rule applies, and of every rule of each smaller priority, at every match of its left side: its `requires` has a result; where that is `true`, its right side has one **and its `ensures` has one, each whatever the other's is** (`ev-en` evaluates both arguments of `ensure`, and `EvalTerm.call` needs a derivation for each);
- inside a function call: of every clause of each priority up to the deciding one, in the text's order up to the first that applies, at every match up to the first whose `requires` holds: its `requires` has a result; of the deciding instance: right side and `ensures` both have one.

The second half of the first bullet is new with C11 and is more than `Eval.ensFails` asks: where the `ensures` is not `true`, `Eval` gives `none` whether or not the right side has any result, and `kore.k` has a value only if the right side's evaluation has one. Soundness is untouched; completeness is silent there (`hdef` false). Evaluating the `ensures` first and the right side only where it is `true` would remove it; that is a `kore.k` change (two equations for `ev-en`), not proposed here: it re-pins the text for a case no fixture has.
