## A closed family on an open sort

`#[k::constructors(sort = Expr, result = KResult)]` says every `Op` variant constructs an `Expr` and that strict evaluation may stop when a child reaches `KResult`.
The `Op` enum is closed—its variants are listed here—while `Expr` remains open to other families and members.

Rust checks variant names and field shapes.
KRS interprets the enum and its attributes as grammar and symbolic constructor declarations; constructing `Add { lhs, rhs }` does not add host integers.

## Syntax templates

Each `#[k::syntax]` string is concrete syntax with placeholders named after fields.
For example, the `Add` template places `lhs` and `rhs` around a literal `+`.
Every field must be represented consistently, and placeholder names are checked against the Rust variant.

## One exhaustive precedence chain

The precedence attribute lists every variant exactly once.
Higher groups bind more tightly than groups below them, so multiplication binds more tightly than addition, which binds more tightly than comparisons and Boolean operators.

`left` makes repeated members of a group left-associative.
The unary group is deliberately bare: a bare group adds no associativity constraint.
Variant source order and blank lines are presentation only; the explicit chain owns precedence.

The `strict` and `seqstrict` attributes visible here are a one-page-ahead preview.
They derive evaluation contexts rather than grammar precedence: `strict` permits any selected child order, while `seqstrict` evaluates selected children from left to right.
The [evaluation chapter](/imp/evaluation) gives them their operational meaning.

## Formatting, avoid, and explicit non-associativity

The checked statement grammar on the [configuration page](/imp/configuration-and-state) supplies the remaining production helpers.
`#[k::format(...)]` controls canonical rendering without changing the parsed term, and the `avoid` flag makes a production lose an otherwise ambiguous parse—for IMP, it resolves the dangling `else`.
Production doc comments become generated grammar documentation.

When same-tier nesting must be rejected, write `non_assoc` explicitly in an enum precedence group.
Bare groups do not mean non-associative.
Cross-family declarations use the reference forms `k::precedence!` and `k::associativity!`, whose exact spellings are indexed in the [language reference](/reference/language).

Missing, duplicated, or unknown precedence members are expansion errors.
Malformed templates are attributed to their authored attribute rather than silently repaired from the Rust enum.

Run `python3 tests/tour-imp/verify.py` from the repository root to check this exact grammar slice, the complete capstone, canonical formatting, deterministic expansion and K emission, and authored source maps.
