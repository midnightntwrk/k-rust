# Matching logic from scratch

Lesson 2 of [A K definition in Lean](/lean).

[Lesson 1](/lean/a-definition-and-its-steps) ended with a question: what is a step, precisely?
K has an official answer.
A K definition, once compiled to KORE, is a theory in matching logic, and a configuration steps to another when the theory says so in every one of its models.
This lesson builds that answer from nothing: what a pattern is, what it means for a pattern to hold, what peano's theory looks like, and how it talks about steps.

The Lean in the panes is the matching-logic library the package builds on (the `MatchingLogic` modules) and the package's translation of KORE into it.

## Patterns denote sets

In first-order logic, a term denotes one element and a formula is true or false.
Matching logic has one kind of thing instead, the pattern, and a pattern denotes a set of elements: the elements that match it.

## What K's meaning gives, and what it does not

This answer to "what is a step" has two properties that matter for the rest of the series.

- It is the meaning K's documentation and papers give a definition: the theory of its KORE, read in matching logic. The package's version of it is in the panes above, and lesson 6 counts its lines.
- It is not something a proof assistant can compute with. "Holds in every model" quantifies over all sets and all interpretations, so even "`run(s(z))` steps to `run(z)`" needs a proof, and none of the peano statements in the last scene has one yet.

So the proofs of lessons 4 to 7 use a second definition of a step, written in Lean as a relation that can be computed with ([lesson 4](/lean/what-a-step-means)).
[Lesson 8](/lean/theorem-a) states the theorem that would make the two definitions agree, Theorem A, and what stands in for it until it is proved.

## What is checked here

- The Lean panes are the files at the recorded commit; the Γ_D panes are the package's own printing of peano's theory, regenerated from its KORE by the package's translator.
- The five-element model of the first two scenes is an illustration. Its sets were computed for this page by the clauses of `Pattern.interpret` shown beside it, not by Lean.
- The peano statements of the last scene are stated in Lean and type-checked; their proofs are `sorry`. The build marks each one as unproved.

## Reproduce

In the kcore-spec package directory:

```sh
lake build
lake exe gammad > GAMMA_D.txt   # peano's Γ_D, printed in the notation of the Γ_D panes
```

The statements of the last scene are in `KcoreSpec/PeanoSpec.lean`; `lake env lean KcoreSpec/PeanoSpec.lean` reports each `sorry`.
