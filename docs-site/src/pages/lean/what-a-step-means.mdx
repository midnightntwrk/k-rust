# What a step means in Lean

Lesson 4 of [A K definition in Lean](/lean).

`run(s(z))` steps to `run(z)` under peano's first rule.
Anyone who wants to rely on that, or on a statement about every step, needs to know what "steps to" means precisely enough for a proof checker: which terms are configurations, when a rule applies, what its right-hand side evaluates to, and what happens when a function has no value.
This lesson reads that meaning from the Lean definitions and runs it on peano.

[Lesson 1](/lean/a-definition-and-its-steps) introduced the question, [lesson 2](/lean/matching-logic) K's official answer in matching logic, and [lesson 3](/lean/the-definition-as-a-value) peano as a Lean value.
This lesson gives the answer the proofs use: a step as a Lean relation.
Read on its own, it needs only what a K rewrite rule is: a left-hand side that matches part of the configuration and a right-hand side that replaces it.

## Five files, read once

The meaning of a step for every K definition is in five Lean files: `FixedSignature.lean` (the sorts and constructors every kompiled definition has), `Core.lean` (what a definition is), `Value.lean` (configurations), `Evaluation.lean` (matching and evaluation) and `Step.lean`.
They import nothing but Lean's `Init` and each other, and they declare no instance, notation or macro.
Every function in them is total and structurally recursive.
Lesson 6 counts what in them a reader must read.

## Functions and right-hand sides

A K function is given by its equations.
kompile emits each as an implication whose premise reads the arguments, and an `[owise]` equation's premise is the negation of all the others'.
The Lean relation `Eval c f vs v` reads them in that way: the first clause whose patterns match decides, the catch-all applies exactly where no clause matches, and nothing else gives a value.

## Why there is no fuel, and where the evaluator is

An evaluator that runs needs a bound on its recursion; a relation does not.
The definitions above are relations, so their meaning contains no bound to review.
The executable evaluator, with its fuel, is in the checked layer, and a theorem proved once says that it computes the relation.

<FuelScene />

So there are two descriptions of a step.
`Step` is what statements are about; `successors` is what the kernel can compute on closed terms, and lesson 5 uses its one-rule form, `stepAt`, to prove facts about particular configurations.
`step_iff` makes either usable in a proof about the other.

## What is machine-checked here

- `step_iff`, `eval_iff` and `Eval.complete` (fuel always suffices) are kernel-checked theorems for every definition that passes `evalReady`; `step_iff_wf` needs only that the definition is well formed.
- `evalReady_peano` is a kernel decision on peano's data.
- The substitutions, values and successors shown in the scenes were computed by Lean on `peanoCore` with the executable evaluator, which these theorems tie to the relation. For `run(s(z)) → run(z)`, `run(z) → done(z)` and the stuck `done(z)`, lesson 5 proves the same facts about `Step` itself.
- Not proved: that a rule of a well-formed definition always rewrites a configuration to a configuration. Until it is, `Step` relates configurations only, by its type, and `successors` filters by sort.

## Reproduce

In the kcore-spec package directory (Lean 4.34.0, no Mathlib):

```sh
lake build        # the library, its checks and the proofs
```

Then put this in a file in the package and run `lake env lean FILE`:

```lean
import KcoreSpec
open KCore KCore.Peano.Ground

-- the substitution of rule runSucc on run(s(z))
#eval matchTerm Peano.peanoCore runSucc.lhs (cfg (run (s z)) ctr0)
-- the value of pred(s(z)) by the executable evaluator
#eval eval Peano.peanoCore "Lblpred" [s z]
-- the successors of run(s(z)), run(z) and done(z)
#eval [cfg (run (s z)) ctr0, cfg (run z) ctr0, cfg (done (natVal z)) ctr0].map fun t =>
  (Value.ofTerm Peano.peanoCore topCellSort t).map fun x => (successors Peano.peanoCore x).map (·.val)
```

The first line prints `some [(0, z), (1, s(z)), (2, <generatedCounter>(0))]` in Lean's notation for the data, and the last prints one successor, one successor and none.
