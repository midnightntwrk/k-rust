# Theorem A

Lesson 8 of [A K definition in Lean](/lean).

The series has given two answers to "what is a step".
[Lesson 2](/lean/matching-logic) gave K's: the steps the theory of the KORE text entails in every model.
[Lesson 4](/lean/what-a-step-means) gave the Lean relation `Step`, and lessons 5 and 7 proved properties of it and made the proofs cheap.
Theorem A is the statement that the two agree.
It is what turns "proved about `Step`" into "proved about the K definition".

## What the next fragment changes

Everything in this series is about one fragment of K, F0: one configuration cell, no builtin arithmetic, no side conditions on rules, no collections.
A draft for the next fragment (several cells, Bool and Int, `requires` clauses, and maps) is written and not yet frozen.
Drafting it changed the meaning of a step in ways a reader should know about:

- with `requires`, evaluating a function can be undefined, and the evaluation relation has to say so for first-match to remain a definition;
- a function is total when its clauses cover every input, not when it has an `[owise]` clause;
- variables range over values of their sort, so `pred_succ` would be stated for every `Nat`, not every ground term;
- the fixed text of a definition depends on what it imports, so the theory of the builtin modules has to be audited before Theorem A can hold for it.

The review cone for peano grows from {draftNumbers.nowTotal} to {draftNumbers.total} lines in the draft, which still carries a converter from the current data.
Theorem A keeps its form; its proof gets harder with each feature.
