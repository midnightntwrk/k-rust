# State of the core

As of kcore-spec `c5815203`, 2026-11-12.
This is the only page of the Lean course that changes as the work moves; the lessons teach only the parts below marked settled.

## Changed since you last read

Newest first.

- The check on the semantics dropped four conditions the theorems never used, among them "every sort has a value". What you must read to know what the theorems say is now about 3,000 lines of Lean. [Lesson 11](/lean/well-formedness) changed for this.
- The three naming conditions that had no example now have one each: a definition that meets every other condition and whose theory has no model.
- KEVM: every sentence of both texts is read or accounted for, except one equation with `ensures` in kompile's text (decision 5). k-rust's text passes the whole well-formedness check. Both meet the check on the semantics when it is computed outside the kernel; inside the kernel, three name conditions run out of the 12 GB memory cap, and work on that is running.
- A package can now be generated from the check on the semantics alone, without the larger well-formedness check.
- Cells with multiplicity `*`, like KEVM's accounts, are read. The text declares each cell a subsort of its collection, which the kernel does not read: the text's own axiom for it is false of the empty map, and no rule in KEVM uses it.
- The short-circuit obligations (decision 1) are used by neither theorem; the decision now only affects separate corollaries about definedness.
- The two theorems now take only the semantics, its decidable check and four statements about its functions; what you must read to know what they say went from about 4,700 to about 3,100 lines of Lean. Lessons 7, 10 and 11 changed for this.
- The reader now refuses a function with an `[owise]` equation beside another equation of priority 200 or more, whether or not the tie is used (decision 2 asks whether to keep refusing).
- A frozen peano statement was corrected. `pred(N) = z for every N that is not s(_)` had no premise that N is a Nat. With `[owise]` as an ordinary clause of priority 200 ([lesson 5](/lean/evaluation)), `pred` has no value at a term that is not a Nat, so the statement is false; it was refuted in the kernel. It now carries the premise that N is a Nat. If you would rather keep the old statement and change the kernel, say so.

## The goal

As of 2026-11-12.

Every K definition we care about, read from kompile's own KORE, gets a Lean package that states soundness and completeness under well-formedness and a list of obligations, and a proof built on top of that package checks.

| Definition | read | well-formed | obligations | sound and complete | used by a proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| imp | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (none named) | ✓ | — |
| KEVM | k-rust's text ✓; kompile's, all but one sentence | computed yes, not yet kernel-checked | listed, open | — | — |
| wasm | — | — | — | — | — |
| mir | — | — | — | — | — |
| K in K | — | — | — | — | — |

- **read**: every sentence of kompile's own KORE for the definition is read, ignored as having no meaning for the semantics, or refused with a reason.
- **well-formed**: the definition passes the well-formedness check, or the failing conditions are listed.
- **obligations**: the facts the definition leaves to its author are listed, and each is proved or named as open.
- **sound and complete**: the generated package states both theorems for the definition.
- **used by a proof**: one existing proof about the definition is rebuilt on the package.

imp is read from kompile 7.1.337's own KORE (1,104 sentences, none refused, none unaccounted for).
One caveat: the package is not tied to the text, so the reader is trusted for it (decision 3).
The peano and fprio examples are also read from kompile's own KORE now.
The other 15 examples are read from k-rust's KORE.

KEVM, measured 2026-11-12: about 400 facts are left to the author. For k-rust's text: 90 functions whose termination no declaration order shows, 37 whose termination certificate is open, 95 `[total]` functions not confirmed to have a value, 46 functions whose clauses of one priority are not confirmed to agree, 41 clauses whose matches are not confirmed to agree, and 69 short-circuit occurrences (decision 1).

## The core, layer by layer

The core is what you must understand to know what the theorems `Sound` and `Complete` say.
Everything else (every proof, the generator, the executable evaluator) is checked by Lean's kernel against it.

| # | Layer | What it says | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Terms | what a configuration is: ground terms, patterns with variables, sorts and symbols as numbers | settled, except literals: they gain bytes and strings with canonical forms |
| 2 | Signature | a definition as a Lean value: sorts, constructors, subsorts, functions as clauses with priorities, rules with priorities, collections | settled, except the library of builtin sorts and symbols (it becomes a list of hook domains) and sort parameters (read by a special case for `ite`) |
| 3 | Values | which ground terms are values of which sort; configurations as well-sorted terms of the top sort | settled |
| 4 | Matching | when a pattern matches a value under a substitution, including map, set and list patterns | settled |
| 5 | Evaluation | function evaluation as a relation: a clause applies where every clause of a smaller priority fails; the order of clauses means nothing | settled, except the builtin operations on Bool and Int (hook domains) and whether Bool operations are strict (decision 1 below) |
| 6 | Step | one step: a rule whose left side matches, whose condition is true, whose right side has a value, and no rule of a smaller priority applies | settled |
| 7 | What functions must do | four statements about evaluation the theorems assume: every function has a result, every `[total]` one a value, clauses of one priority agree, matches of one clause agree | settled |
| 8 | Matching logic | patterns, models and entailment, as much as the statements use | settled |
| 9 | The theory Γ | the theory a definition denotes, built from the Lean value: sorts, constructors, collections, function equations, rule axioms | settled, except the axioms of the builtin domains |
| 10 | Sound and Complete | every step is entailed by the theory, which has a model; every successor the theory forces is a step; the two theorems, with their two hypotheses | settled |
| 11 | Well-formedness | the check on the semantics both theorems assume: well-sorted subsorts, clauses and rules, the conditions the theory needs to have a model, and names apart | settled, except the first half of one naming condition (every symbol named like `ite` is named at a numbered sort) |

## What you trust

1. Lean's kernel and its three standard axioms.
2. The definitions the theorems are stated with: the step relation, evaluation, matching, the theory Γ and its step closure, the check on the semantics (`coreOK`), the four statements about functions (`FnHyp`), and entailment in matching logic. Reading the course is reviewing them.
3. The builtin domains: the Bool and Int operations as defined in Lean. Bytes, String and the others are parameters with no interpretation.
4. The reader of a KORE file, for a definition read without the tie: that the Lean definition it produces is the one the text describes. With the tie, the kernel checks that printing the definition back gives the text, up to the order of its sentences.
5. kompile's translation from K to KORE. Nothing here speaks about the K source.

Not trusted: the generator, the deciders, the executable evaluator and k-rust. The kernel checks each against the definitions.

## Open decisions

Each is a question you can answer by replying.

1. **Strict Bool operations.** Today `A andBool B` has a value only when both `A` and `B` have one, and a definition that relies on short-circuiting carries an obligation per place it does. imp-sec's six such obligations are false, and parts of KEVM carry more, so those definitions would have to be rewritten with `ite` or guarded clauses. It no longer touches soundness or completeness. Keep the strict reading? (Recommended: yes.)
2. **A priority of 200 or above beside `[owise]`.** `[owise]` is priority 200, so a function with an `[owise]` clause and a clause of priority 200 or above is refused today. The KORE text puts the `[owise]` equation after every other equation, while the kernel reads `[owise]` as priority 200, and the two differ wherever both equations apply. Keep refusing, or read a priority of 200 or above as "after `[owise]`" and state where that differs from the KORE text?
3. **Definitions read without the tie.** The theory Γ is built from the Lean definition, and the tie, a kernel check that the definition prints back as its KORE text, is optional. Without it the reader is trusted. Accept that, or have a package read without the tie say so in the names of its theorems?
4. **The goal and its order.** Is the scoreboard above the end goal? The proposed order: read every definition from kompile's own KORE, starting with KEVM; rebuild one existing proof on a small package; review the core through this course; compare the executable evaluator with recorded outputs; then K in K.
5. **An `ensures` on a function equation.** One KEVM equation carries one. Proposed: read the equation without it and list the claim as stated by the text but not part of the reading; such a definition cannot be tied to its text. Agree?
