## Cause

Verified unless marked.

Stream cells and the rules that reach the hook.
The four executing definitions declare `<input color="magenta" stream="stdin"> .List </input>` and `<output color="brown" stream="stdout"> .List </output>` (`simple-untyped.md:394-395`, `simple-typed-dynamic.md:202-203`, `kool-untyped.md:331-332`, `kool-typed-dynamic.md:211-212`; `kool-typed-static.md:304` declares only the output cell), and none of them mentions `$IO`, `"on"`, `"off"`, `#buffer` or a `#write`/`#getc` application (grep over the four sources).
`print` appends each value to the tail of `<output>` (`simple-untyped.md:872`, `kool-untyped.md:493`) and `read()` takes `ListItem(I:Int)` from the head of `<input>` (`simple-untyped.md:742`, `kool-untyped.md:471`).
The port's `resolve_io` pass instantiates K's stream modules for these cells exactly as the Java `ResolveIOStreams` does (`crates/k-rust/src/kompile/passes/resolve_io.rs:48-57,90-127`; `k/k-frontend/src/main/java/org/kframework/compile/ResolveIOStreams.java:49-85`; detailed in `workers/CB-04.md` "Cause"), so every hook application comes from `k/k-distribution/include/kframework/builtin/domains.md`:

- initializers `<stdin> ListItem(#buffer($STDIN:String)) ListItem($IO:String) ListItem(#istream(#stdin))` (`:2722`) and `<stdout> ListItem(#ostream(#stdout)) ListItem($IO:String) ListItem(#buffer(""))` (`:2803`);
- `stdoutBufferFloat/Int/String` (`:2805-2835`, priority 25) move a printed item into `#buffer` and match the `$IO` slot as `ListItem(_)`, so they apply under `"on"` and `"off"` alike;
- `stdoutWrite` (`:2838-2846`, priority 30) matches `ListItem("on")` and a non-empty buffer and rewrites `#ostream(N)` to `{#write(N, S) ~> N:Int}:>Int` while emptying the buffer; it is the only rule that empties `#buffer` and the only source of `#write`;
- `stdinGetc` (`:2725-2733`) matches `ListItem("on")` and appends `chrChar(#getc(N))`; `stdinParseString`, `stdinParseInt` and `stdinTrim` (`:2736-2765`) serve values from whatever the buffer holds, with no `$IO` condition; `stdinUnblock` (`:2782-2787`) is the template the pass instantiates for the `read()` rule;
- `#getc`, `#read`, `#putc`, `#write` are `[function, hook(IO.*), impure]` (`:2553-2554,2563-2564`); `#write` returns `.K` on success (`:2558-2560`); `#EOF` is `#getc`'s end-of-file result (`:2431`).

The `$IO` switch.
K's `krun` sets `io=false` only for `--search` (`k/k-distribution/src/main/scripts/bin/krun:442-448`), passes `$IO` as `"on"`/`"off"` (`:545-551`) and, under `off`, buffers the whole standard input into `$STDIN` with trailing newlines collapsed to one (`:553-566`).
krust krun does the same: `--io` defaults to `on` unless `--search` (`crates/k-rust/src/main.rs:520-524`, `:2241`), injects `$IO` (`:2243-2248`) and `$STDIN` (`:2249-2263`) with `buffered_stdin_text(read_stdin_for_stream())` under `off` (`:4250-4270`) and an empty `$STDIN` under `on` or when the program itself is read from stdin (`program_uses_stdin`, `:2102-2105,2156`).
The driver forwards a recipe's `--io` unchanged (`run.py:1381`); no tutorial recipe passes one.
Under `on` every plain execution therefore fires `stdoutWrite` at its first `print`, and the port's simplifier meets `#write(1, S)` on two constructor-like arguments.

No evaluator.
`crates/k-rust-backend/src/builtin.rs:215` dispatches `IO.logString` only; every other `IO.*` hook falls to `BuiltinResult::Unsupported(UnsupportedHookReason::NotImplemented)` (`:228-230`, message `:92-93`), which `simplify.rs:151-154` renders as the recorded error and `rewrite.rs:600-606` turns into a `HaltReason::Simplification` leaf.
The inventory lists `IO.write`, `IO.getc`, `IO.read`, `IO.putc` and fifteen other IO hooks as unsupported with the reason `host filesystem, process, clock, and stream effects are not exposed by the backend` (`crates/k-rust/tests/fixtures/hook-capabilities.toml:214-236`), and `crates/k-rust/tests/hook_capabilities.rs:140` (`pinned_prelude_hooks_have_an_enforced_capability_classification`) checks that each unsupported hook errors on constructor-like arguments and is not treated as symbolic (`:207-225`).
This is the contracted outcome (`docs/compatibility.md:19-21`).

What the port's effect model can and cannot carry today.
The only effect is `BuiltinEffect::UserLog(String)` (`builtin.rs:102-104`); the simplifier turns an `Effect` result into a `.K` term via `builtin_effect_result` (`simplify.rs:2344-2351`, `:2446-2468`, hard-wired to `IO.logString`) and returns the effect in `Simplification::effects` (`:99,107`).
The rewriter and the search record effects through `record_effects` (`rewrite.rs:1150-1158`, `search.rs:1662`), which calls the caller's observer at recording time: after the loop-head simplification of each pending state (`rewrite.rs:514`) and after each `RewriteResult::Finished` (`:620`, also `:873-875,908-910,929`; `search.rs:430-432,547,571`).
The observers are `eprintln!` on the krun execution path (`main.rs:3048-3050`) and a post-hoc loop over `result.effects` on the search path (`:2996-2998`); the RPC wire carries `EffectOutput::UserLog` (`crates/k-rust/src/backend/wire.rs:185-186`, `:529-531`), and observation attribution names `builtin:IO.logString` explicitly (`crates/k-rust-backend/src/transition.rs:259-266`).
The execution explores every branch unless `--execute-to-branch` (`ExecutionBranchMode::ExploreAll`, `main.rs:3035-3039`), and effects are recorded per pending state, so an effect performed on a branch that later ends as a residual leaf is observed exactly like one on the terminal path.
The collatz reproduction above shows what that means for console output: four residual leaves each carry a prefix of the output, so a real `IO.write` performed at recording time would have written `Testing Collatz' conjecture up to what number? ` five times.
Whether a state in `discarded` (`rewrite.rs:406`) has its effects observed before it is discarded is unverified; the recording points above make it likely.

What each pinned reference does.
K's `krun` runs the LLVM interpreter with the result directed to a file (`krun:630`), then prints that file through `kore-print --output "$outputMode"` (`:714`); under `--output none` nothing is printed, and the only bytes on `krun`'s stdout are those the interpreter itself wrote to its inherited descriptor 1 through the LLVM runtime's `IO.write`, which the recipe pipes into `diff - $@.out`.
The LLVM runtime source is not in the sandbox (`k/llvm-backend/src/main/native/llvm-backend` is empty, the nix store's `lib/kframework/llvm-backend` is empty, and the kompiled `interpreter` binaries are stripped), so the implementation of `hook_IO_write` is unverified here; its contract is the one `domains.md:2558-2564` states.
The pinned Kore engine registers a single IO builtin, `IO.logString` (`haskell-backend/kore/src/Kore/Builtin/IO.hs:75-78`, `evalLogString` returning `dotk` at `:80-96`), and the Booster has no IO builtin module (`haskell-backend/booster/library/Booster/Builtin/` holds `BOOL`, `Base`, `INT`, `KEQUAL`, `LIST`, `MAP`).
`docs/compatibility.md` "Backend scope" (`:12-21`) says LLVM-specific runtime behavior is outside the contract, that an LLVM expected output cannot define the behavior of a hook that neither pinned Kore engine evaluates (`:16`), and that a hook without evaluator or equation must report the unsupported-hook error on constructor-like arguments (`:19-21`); "CLI scope" (`:129-137`) says krust does not implement K's flag surface for interchangeability and an untranslatable flag must stay unsupported; "Driver scope" (`:139-147`) makes the driver responsible for translating recipes into supported operations; "Comparison contract" (`:149-175`) makes `scripts/reference-normalisations.toml` the register of every permitted equivalence (`N1`–`N30`, `C1`–`C8`).

What K's own semantics say the `.out` is.
By the stream rules, the bytes written to descriptor 1 during an `on` execution are exactly the successive contents of `#buffer` that `stdoutWrite` flushes, in flush order, and each flush is the concatenation of the items `print` appended since the previous flush; under `off` the same items are appended by the same priority-25 rules, nothing flushes, and the final `#buffer` is their concatenation.
The only rules that touch `#buffer` are the five stream rules, and none of the four definitions inspects `$IO`, so the output of an execution is a function of the definition's rules and the program, not of the hook: the hook only transports it.
K relies on exactly this equivalence when it forces `off` for `--search` and `--debug` (`krun:442-448`, `domains.md:2775-2780`), and the tutorials' own search recipes read the result from `#buffer(S:String)` (`1_simple/1_untyped/Makefile:14-20`, `2_kool/2_typed/2_static/Makefile:8-9`).
On the input side the equivalence holds when the bytes the `on` execution would pull through `#getc(0)` equal the `$STDIN` buffer; `buffered_stdin_text` (`main.rs:4258-4270`, mirroring `krun:557-558`) collapses trailing newlines to one, and all 17 `.in` files of the four cases end in exactly one `\n` (checked with `od`), so the buffers are the piped bytes.
End of input differs in form (`#EOF` from `#getc` versus an exhausted buffer that no parse rule serves) but both leave the program stuck, and no tutorial program reads past its `.in`.
Interleaving of prompts and reads is not observable through a pipe into `diff`.

The reproductions confirm both halves: `hello-world.kool` under `off` yields the `.out` bytes in the buffer; `collatz.simple` under `off` with its `.in` yields the `.out` bytes in one leaf, with the residual leaves of CB-08 residue 3 beside it.

## Decision

The choice is the owner's; this section costs the three options and recommends one.

Option (a): step-level `llvm-only` exclusions as in CB-04.
It needs 98 `step_exclusions` rows (selectors `test` and `out` as recorded) across four cases, plus the three CB-04 rows.
It removes every execution step of SIMPLE untyped (20 of 23 plain), SIMPLE typed dynamic (21 of 24), KOOL untyped (28 of 28) and KOOL typed dynamic (29 of 29) from the evidence, leaving the kompile step and the search recipes; `matching_step_exclusion` (`scripts/conformance/ratchet.py:176-193`) matches the first failing step only, so with the rows in place a regression in any later program is masked while the first excluded program still fails (the limitation CB-04 recorded).
A case-level `llvm-only` would be more honest about coverage, since the excluded set is nearly the whole case, but it would also hide a kompile regression.
Either form applies `docs/compatibility.md:16` to `.out` files that, by the analysis above, do not define the hook's behavior at all; the sentence covers a hook's semantics, not a configuration cell the definition's own rules compute.
Cost hours; consequence: no execution coverage of the language tutorials for as long as the rows stand, and the decision reverses itself the day the owner wants console output.

Option (b): a backend IO evaluator from first principles.
The contract for the four hooks is: `#write(fd, S)` and `#putc(fd, C)` perform the write and return `.K`; `#getc(fd)` returns the next byte as `Int` or `#EOF`; `#read(fd, n)` returns up to `n` bytes as `String`; all are `impure`, so each application is performed once, in the order the rewriting commits it.
File-level work:
`builtin.rs` gains an IO namespace evaluator (`:215-231`) with `BuiltinEffect` variants that carry a result term for the reads (`:102-104`), which means `evaluate_builtin` needs an effect context (a handle to the process's descriptors 0, 1, 2 and the driver-fed stdin) that the current pure signature does not have; `simplify.rs:2446-2468` generalises `builtin_effect_result`; `transition.rs:259-266` generalises attribution.
The ordering guarantee is the design item: effects are observed at recording time on every explored branch (`rewrite.rs:514,620`), so real writes need either a single-successor concrete mode (the first applicable rule in priority order, then declaration order, which is what `docs/compatibility.md:122` already documents as the port's choice) in which `--io on` is the only mode that performs effects, or a deferral of effects to the selected terminal leaf's path; reads cannot be deferred at all, because the value of `#getc(0)` must be known to continue rewriting, so `--io on` with reads is single-path by necessity.
`main.rs` needs a way to keep program bytes apart from the KORE result (today the KORE goes to stdout and `UserLog` to stderr, `:3048-3050`), for example printing the result to a file or a `--output none` equivalent, which touches the CLI-scope sentence (`docs/compatibility.md:132-134`); `wire.rs:185-186,529-531` gains effect variants, and the RPC path must keep reporting the unsupported hook because the Booster oracle has no IO.
`--io off` must keep buffering exactly as now (`main.rs:2249-2263`, `:4258-4270`), since the search recipes depend on it.
The inventory (`hook-capabilities.toml:214-236`) and `hook_capabilities.rs:140` move the four hooks to `implemented`; `docs/compatibility.md:16` needs an amendment stating that an LLVM console output may define descriptor 0/1/2 behavior for these four hooks, which is the reversal Decision 12-2 left to the owner (`draft/fable51-review/design/DECISIONS-NEEDED.toml:452-462`); the driver replaces completion-only with a byte comparison of krust stdout against the `.out` (`run.py:1441-1447`) and fixes the empty-stdout classification (`:1417-1419`).
Regression tests: a backend test per hook on a one-rule stream-cell definition asserting bytes and result; an exactly-once test on a definition whose execution branches and whose residual leaf would repeat the write; a CLI test that the KORE result and program bytes do not interleave; a driver probe.
Re-estimate: 3 to 4 weeks (the single-path mode and effect-context plumbing are one to two weeks on their own; the rest is days each), plus the same throughput dependency as (c).
Consequence: krust gains console IO as a product feature, at the price of a contract amendment that makes the LLVM runtime the oracle for one namespace.

Option (c): driver-side normalisation, run under `--io off` and compare the stdout stream cell's buffer with the `.out`.
Soundness follows from the stream rules as stated in Cause: the buffer under `off` is the concatenation of the bytes `on` writes, the input buffer equals the piped bytes for every `.in` of the corpus, no definition inspects `$IO`, and the equivalence is the one K itself relies on for search.
The driver would, for a plain-krun recipe whose translated flags contain `--output none` and whose `.out` is non-empty, run krust with `--io off` (`run.py:1386-1390` sets `completion_only`; the `extra` flags at `:1381` take `--io`), read the final KORE it already logs, select the terminal leaf, locate the stream cell whose contents are `ListItem(#ostream(1)) ListItem("off") ListItem(#buffer(S))` (from the KORE, not from a `--pattern`: the pattern path hangs on KOOL untyped, table above), unescape the KORE string literal, and compare bytes with the `.out`, recording a new comparison label; a recipe whose `.out` is empty keeps completion-only, and a krust result of `\bottom` must stop counting as completion.
The residual leaves of CB-08 residue 3 mean the terminal-leaf selection must be strict: exactly one leaf may be free of residual constraints and hold the buffer; several candidates or none is a `mismatch` whose divergence lists the leaves, so that residue 3 stays visible instead of being absorbed.
Programs that read stdin already get the `.in` through the driver (`run.py:189`), and `--io off` buffers it (`main.rs:2252-2256`).
Nondeterministic programs (`threads_07.simple`, `threads.kool` where run) may print in an order the LLVM interpreter did not choose; that is a genuine difference to record, exactly as under (b).
Register the comparison as `C9` in `scripts/reference-normalisations.toml` (`[[conformance]]`, gate `conformance krun`, anchor `scripts/conformance/run.py:compare_stdout_buffer`), add a paragraph to `docs/compatibility.md` "Comparison contract" and one sentence to "Driver scope", and keep "Backend scope" unchanged: `IO.write` remains unsupported, `--io on` executions keep halting as `:19-21` require, and no LLVM output defines the hook.
Cost: days for driver, register, docs and tests; consequence: the four tutorials' 98 executions become comparable now, the residue-3 leaves and the thread-order differences become measured mismatches, and the corpus's completion cost lands on CB-08.

Recommendation: (c), with the driver-soundness fix for `\bottom` and non-empty `.out` under completion-only folded in, and (a) kept only as the per-program fallback for executions that cannot finish inside the budget after CB-08.
It follows the owner's stated preference: the port implements nothing that imitates the LLVM runtime, the differential boundary gets a normalisation whose justification is K's own stream semantics, and (b) stays a separate product decision that (c) neither blocks nor prejudges; if the owner takes (b) later, the byte comparison of (c) is the test it needs.
