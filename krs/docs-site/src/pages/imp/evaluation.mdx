## Anchor rules at the computation head

`for Expr;` constrains every clause to an expression at the head of the computation cell.
KRS completes that cell context and frames the remaining K sequence.
The block-local `use Op::*` makes constructor patterns readable without changing their Rust identities.

Each named clause becomes an independent unordered transition rule.
The aligned table is presentation: moving `eval_add` above or below `eval_mul` does not create priority.

## Symbolic builtin applications

`int::add(lhs, rhs)` and `bool::not(value)` construct builtin applications in the object language.
They are typed by Rust-visible marker shapes, but K evaluates them through the selected semantic backend—not by calling a Rust function during collection.

## Patterns and narrowing

Unadorned names such as `lhs` and `rhs` bind symbolic subterms.
Literals such as `true` match object-language values, and `..` discards unneeded fields.
A name repeated within one pattern is one binder and therefore imposes nonlinear symbolic equality; it does not bind two unrelated values.

The equality constructors accept both integers and Booleans.
Their clauses would overlap without the `where lhs: Bool` or `where lhs: Int` narrowing constraints, which refine binder sorts using the symbolic sort graph.

Strictness determines where evaluation may occur; rules determine what an evaluated redex becomes.
If no rule and no derived context applies, the term is stuck rather than implicitly delegated to Rust.

Misspelled constructors and impossible builtin operands fail through Rust shape checking.
Unbound variables, inconsistent binder sorts, or overlapping semantic heads fail during KRS parsing or analysis.
