## Read both cells at once

`for Machine;` makes each clause a configuration rule.
The left side of `var` requires an identifier at the head of `k` and a matching `name -> value` entry in `env`.
The right side replaces only that computation head.

The omitted environment cell and the `..` sequence tail and map tail are frames: they persist unchanged.
This is one simultaneous symbolic rewrite, not a series of Rust field assignments.

## Persistent map updates

`assign` matches an evaluated assignment at the computation head and binds the complete environment as `e`.
Its result erases the consumed head item and replaces `env` with the symbolic application `map::update(e, name, value)`.

Map lookup in a pattern and persistent update on a result are different operations.
Neither reads or mutates a host `HashMap` during definition collection.

`where name: Id` and `where value: Value` narrow binders to the sorts required by the cell and builtin operations.
Clause source order remains semantically irrelevant.
The clause names identify the authored rule sentences; constructor and cell identities still come from their Rust-resolved declarations.

An unknown cell, incompatible frame, duplicate binder with inconsistent meaning, or statically overlapping head fails analysis.
A well-formed rule whose pattern is absent is merely inapplicable.
